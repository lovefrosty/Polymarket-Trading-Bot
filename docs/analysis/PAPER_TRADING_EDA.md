# Paper-Trading EDA: What the Saved Evidence Actually Shows

Analysis date: August 6, 2026

Primary artifact: `tmp/core_mm_runs/btc-paper-20260315T222135Z`

Evidence type: historical local simulation against public Polymarket book data

## Bottom line

The best committed run is a useful engineering result, but it is not enough to
claim a durable trading edge or consistent income. It recorded **$23.93 in
realized net PnL**, **$17.11 in ending total PnL**, and a **$6.53 maximum
drawdown** over 33.1 minutes. However, 92% of its fills and 95% of its realized
net PnL came from the first of only three market windows.

That concentration makes the sensible next experiment narrower: one BTC
15-minute market at a time, repeated across many independent windows with the
same frozen configuration and current fee model.

The first contract generated 173 of 188 fills and $22.75 of $23.93 realized
net PnL. The next two produced eight and seven fills and $0.85 and $0.33,
respectively. The result may reflect one favorable path as much as a repeatable
strategy.

Across the other six committed run summaries, four recorded zero fills; the
remaining two recorded only 23 and 12 fills. Those runs are diagnostics, not a
large independent sample.

## Sharpe ratio

**Not estimated.** A conventional Sharpe ratio would be misleading here.
There are only three market windows, one dominates the result, and the 1,438
cycle-level observations are overlapping marks from the same inventory path.
Annualizing those observations would turn serially correlated simulator noise
into a precise-looking but invalid statistic.

A defensible estimate needs returns grouped into independent, predeclared
market windows, a fixed capital denominator, enough out-of-sample windows to
estimate dispersion, and no configuration selection using the evaluation set.

## Risk of ruin

**Not estimated.** Risk of ruin requires a credible loss distribution and a
specified bankroll, sizing rule, loss boundary, and dependence model. This
repository currently has neither enough independent losing windows nor a saved
artifact for the reported approximately $25 live loss. Treating that loss as
zero-risk or reverse-engineering a ruin probability from memory would be
misleading.

For a future estimate, retain every run—including failures—and bootstrap
market-window outcomes in blocks. Report ruin probabilities across multiple
queue, slippage, fee, and adverse-selection scenarios at the intended bankroll
and order-size settings.

## Data and accounting notes

- The PnL tape repeats portfolio-level realized PnL once per token. The analysis
  deduplicates realized PnL by timestamp and sums token-level unrealized PnL.
- Per-market realized PnL is attributed from each fill's
  `realized_net_pnl_delta` field.
- The historical run charged 25 bps on modeled touch fills. Current paper and
  venue-specific fee logic has changed, so the chart is historical rather than
  a forecast under today's configuration.
- Fills are generated by the local simulator. They are not exchange receipts
  and do not validate queue priority, cancel latency, hidden liquidity, or
  production adverse selection.
- The absence of a committed live-loss tape prevents a direct paper-versus-live
  reconciliation.

## Reproduce the analysis

From the repository root:

```bash
python scripts/analyze_paper_runs.py
python -m pytest -q tests/scripts/test_analyze_paper_runs.py
```

The script regenerates both PNG files from the committed JSONL tapes and prints
the exact metrics and per-market attribution as JSON.

## Next evidence gate

Freeze one Kalshi BTC market-selection and quoting profile before collecting
new results. Run it across at least 30 independent 15-minute windows and retain
zero-fill and failed runs. Use dynamic Kalshi fees, delayed/partial fills,
cancel latency, and conservative queue assumptions. Evaluate once on untouched
windows, then compare the paper fills with a separately approved tiny canary.

Until that work is complete, the correct conclusion is: **the system has a
promising historical paper path, but profitability, Sharpe, risk of ruin, and
consistent income remain unproven.**
